## Credit risk
Issuers rated below investment grade default more often than investment-grade issuers, and holders usually recover only part of the face value.

## Liquidity risk
High-yield bonds trade less frequently. In stressed markets bid-ask spreads widen and selling quickly can require a large discount.

## Interest rate risk
When market interest rates rise, prices of fixed-rate bonds fall. Sensitivity is measured by **duration**, not by maturity. Duration is the weighted-average time to receive the bond's cash flows; for a coupon-paying bond it is always shorter than the time to maturity, and the higher the coupon, the shorter the duration. As an approximation, price change ≈ −duration × change in rates. High-yield bonds carry high coupons, so their duration is typically well below their maturity.
